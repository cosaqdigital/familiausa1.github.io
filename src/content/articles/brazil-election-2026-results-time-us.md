**Updated October 28, 2026:** Brazil's presidential runoff is over, and the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) has announced the final result.

**Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT) was reelected president with 64,256,232 votes, or 51.79% of valid votes. Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) finished second with 59,826,041 votes, or 48.21%.**

This page originally helped U.S.-based readers understand when Brazil's runoff results would begin. It now keeps that time-zone guide while adding the **final 2026 Brazil election result, turnout, election-night timeline and official TSE sources**.

## Brazil election 2026 final results

According to the TSE's official post-election announcement, the presidential runoff finished as follows:

| Candidate | Party | Votes | Share of valid votes |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva | PT | 64,256,232 | 51.79% |
| Flávio Bolsonaro | PL | 59,826,041 | 48.21% |

The margin between the two candidates was **4,430,191 votes**.

The TSE also reported that more than **158 million voters were eligible** for the second round, about **128.2 million people voted**, and abstention was approximately **19.2%**.

Official source: [TSE — President announces Lula's reelection and reviews the 2026 runoff](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/presidente-do-tse-anuncia-reeleicao-de-lula-e-faz-balanco-do-2o-turno-das-eleicoes-2026).

## Who won Brazil's 2026 presidential election?

**Lula won the 2026 presidential runoff.**

The final TSE figures gave Lula **51.79%** of valid votes and Flávio Bolsonaro **48.21%**.

That result returned Lula to office for another presidential term after a runoff that was much closer than the early partial count shown during election night.

## What happened on election night?

The normal 2026 election calendar scheduled second-round voting from **8:00 AM to 5:00 PM Brasília time** on October 25.

Under ordinary circumstances, polling-station reports can begin to be issued after voting closes.

Election night did not follow the normal timetable exactly.

The TSE announced that voting would continue **only in locations where it had not been completed by 5:00 PM**, following a decision by Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin. The TSE said it would resume:

- receiving Boletins de Urna, or polling-station reports;
- totalizing the votes; and
- publishing the results

**after 7:00 PM Brasília time**.

Official source: [TSE clarification on the extension of voting](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/esclarecimento-sobre-prorrogacao-da-votacao).

## Why did Brazil's election results pause?

The pause was not a change to the votes already cast.

It was an operational decision connected to the fact that voting was still underway in some locations after the scheduled 5:00 PM closing time.

The TSE said that, while those locations remained open, the receipt of polling-station reports, vote totalization and public result publication would resume after 7:00 PM Brasília time.

A later TSE report said the extension applied to sections that were still operating after 5:00 PM and that votes continued to be recorded during the extended period.

That explains why people following the election from the United States may have expected updates around the normal poll-closing time but then saw a longer wait before national totals continued moving.

## What time were polls originally supposed to close?

Brazil's official 2026 election calendar set second-round voting for **8:00 AM to 5:00 PM Brasília time**.

The calendar also provided for polling-station reports to be issued after 5:00 PM as voting closed.

Official source: [TSE 2026 election calendar](https://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/calendario-eleitoral).

The October 25 extension was therefore an election-day exception, not the standard schedule published in advance.

## How does Brazil publish election results?

Brazil's electronic voting system produces a **Boletim de Urna (BU)** at the polling-station level after voting concludes.

Those records are transmitted and processed for totalization.

The TSE's results system lets the public follow the count as polling-station data is received and processed, including candidate totals, percentages and the number of sections totalized.

The official TSE results service also provides access to election records that can be used to verify the published totals.

Official source: [TSE — how to follow vote totalization](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/saiba-como-acompanhar-a-totalizacao-dos-votos-apos-as-17h).

## How many people voted in Brazil's 2026 runoff?

The TSE's post-election review reported:

- **more than 158 million eligible voters**;
- approximately **128.2 million voters who participated**; and
- an abstention rate of about **19.2%**.

The TSE said the second-round abstention rate was lower than the **21.08%** recorded in the first round.

These numbers describe overall participation in the second round, while the presidential percentages above are calculated from **valid votes**.
