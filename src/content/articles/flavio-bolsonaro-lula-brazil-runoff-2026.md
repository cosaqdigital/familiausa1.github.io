Brazil's 2026 presidential election will be decided in a runoff between **Flávio Bolsonaro (PL)** and **Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT)**.

The second round is scheduled for **Sunday, October 25, 2026**.

For English-language live results on election day, use our [Brazil Election 2026 live results page](/brazil-election-2026.html).

This article focuses on the two candidates, the official first-round numbers and what those numbers do — and do not — tell us about the runoff.

## Official first-round results

Brazil's Superior Electoral Court reported that, with 99.99% of polling stations counted:

| Candidate | Party | Votes | Valid-vote share |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Flávio Bolsonaro | PL | 56,104,268 | 47.03% |
| Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva | PT | 53,876,617 | 45.16% |

The TSE also reported 125,272,513 valid votes in the first round.

Official source: [TSE — Flávio Bolsonaro and Lula advance to the second round](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/flavio-bolsonaro-e-lula-vao-disputar-o-2o-turno-para-a-presidencia-da-republica).

## Who is Flávio Bolsonaro?

The TSE describes Flávio Bolsonaro as a lawyer, former state deputy and current senator for Rio de Janeiro.

He is running for president for the first time in 2026.

His party is the **Liberal Party (PL)**, and his vice-presidential running mate is federal deputy **Alfredo Gaspar**.

For readers outside Brazil, the important election-night detail is that candidate names and party abbreviations will appear in the official count. Our English page preserves those official identifiers while translating the surrounding interface.

## Who is Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva?

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, commonly known as **Lula**, is the incumbent president and is seeking reelection.

The TSE notes that he previously served as president from 2003 to 2010 and returned to office for the 2023-2026 term.

His party is the **Workers' Party (PT)**, and his vice-presidential running mate is **Geraldo Alckmin**.

## Who led the first round?

Flávio Bolsonaro finished first in the official first-round count with **47.03% of valid votes**.

Lula finished second with **45.16%**.

The difference between them was about 2.23 million votes in the totals reported by the TSE.

But that does not create a vote advantage that carries into the runoff.

## Does the first-round result carry over?

No.

The October 25 runoff is a new election.

The first-round result determines which two candidates advance, but the second-round vote decides the presidency.

That means both candidates begin the runoff with no inherited vote total from October 4.

Voters cast a new ballot in the second round.

## Why was a second round required?

Neither candidate secured the presidency in the first round, so the two most-voted candidates advanced.

Brazil's two-round system is designed to narrow the presidential contest to two candidates when the first round does not produce a winner.

## When will the runoff happen?

The official TSE calendar schedules the second round for **October 25, 2026**.

Voting is scheduled from **8:00 AM to 5:00 PM Brasília time**.

After voting closes, polling-station reports can begin to be issued and official results can be processed.

Official source: [TSE 2026 election calendar](https://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/eleicoes-2026/calendario-eleitoral).

## What should readers watch during the count?

Three numbers matter most:

1. each candidate's total votes;
2. each candidate's percentage of valid votes;
3. how much of the official count has been processed.

An early lead can change as more votes are included.
