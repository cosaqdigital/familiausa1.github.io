Brazil will hold a **presidential runoff on Sunday, October 25, 2026**, after no candidate won the presidency in the first round. For American and international readers, the process can look unusual because Brazil uses a national election authority and a two-round system for the presidency.

The most important thing to know is simple: the runoff is a **new election between the two candidates who advanced from the first round**. The first-round percentages do not carry over.

## Why does Brazil have a presidential runoff?

Brazilian presidential elections can require two rounds.

If no candidate reaches the threshold needed to win in the first round, the two most-voted candidates advance to a second round.

For the 2026 election, Brazil's Superior Electoral Court confirmed that **Flávio Bolsonaro of the Liberal Party (PL)** and **Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of the Workers' Party (PT)** advanced to the runoff.

According to the TSE's official first-round report, Flávio Bolsonaro received **47.03% of valid votes** and Lula received **45.16%**.

Those figures explain how the runoff was reached, but they do not determine the second-round result.

Official source: [Brazil's Superior Electoral Court — TSE](https://www.tse.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/flavio-bolsonaro-e-lula-vao-disputar-o-2o-turno-para-a-presidencia-da-republica).

## When is the second round?

The second round is scheduled for **October 25, 2026**.

The official TSE election calendar states that voting begins at **8:00 AM Brasília time** and closes at **5:00 PM Brasília time**. After voting closes, polling-station reports can begin to be issued and the official count can be published as data is processed.

That centralized national schedule is one of the main differences American readers will notice when comparing Brazil with U.S. elections.

Official source: [TSE 2026 election calendar](https://www.tse.jus.br/eleicoes/eleicoes-2026/calendario-eleitoral).

## Who can win the runoff?

Only the two presidential candidates who advanced from the first round appear in the presidential runoff.

The runoff decides the presidency based on the second-round vote. It is not an extension of the first-round tally.

In practical terms, voters return to the polls and choose between the two remaining presidential tickets.

## How does Brazil count votes?

Brazil uses electronic voting machines, and election results are published nationally through the electoral justice system.

After polls close, polling stations produce election reports. Those results are transmitted and processed through the official election infrastructure.

The TSE is the national authority responsible for publishing the official totals.

## When will results start appearing?

The official election calendar says voting closes at **5:00 PM Brasília time**.

That does not mean a complete national result appears at exactly 5:00 PM. It means the reporting phase begins after voting closes and official data starts being processed.

The percentage counted will increase over time as more polling-station data is included.

## Does an early lead mean a candidate has won?

No.

An early lead only describes the votes included in the count at that moment.

As more votes are processed, percentages can change.

## Is the TSE similar to a U.S. election authority?

Not exactly.

The United States has a decentralized election system in which state and local authorities play major roles in administration and reporting.

Brazil's election reporting is much more nationally centralized through the electoral justice system.

For an American reader, this difference is important because it explains why Brazil can present a national presidential count through one central election authority.

## What happened in the first round?

The first round took place on **October 4, 2026**.

With 99.99% of polling stations counted in the official TSE report cited above:

- Flávio Bolsonaro received 56,104,268 votes, or 47.03% of valid votes;
- Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva received 53,876,617 votes, or 45.16% of valid votes.

No candidate won the presidency in the first round, so the top two advanced.

Again, the runoff starts with a new vote. Those first-round totals are context, not a starting balance.
